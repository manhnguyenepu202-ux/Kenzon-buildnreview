Good photos sell prints. On Etsy, Shopify and Pinterest, shoppers judge your product from a thumbnail, so clean, honest photos and a short video do more for sales than any discount. You do not need a camera: a recent phone, a window and a few cheap props are enough. Below is the setup, the shot list and the video formats we would use for 3D-printed and laser-engraved products.

## 1. Set up a simple photo corner

- **Light:** shoot next to a large window with indirect daylight. Avoid direct sun, which makes hard shadows and blows out white filament.
- **Diffuse it:** tape baking paper or a white shower curtain over the window, or use a cheap foldable lightbox for small parts.
- **Bounce it:** a sheet of white foam board on the shadow side fills in dark areas.
- **Background:** a curved sheet of white or light grey card (a "sweep") for catalogue shots; wood, linen or a desk for lifestyle shots.

## 2. Phone settings that matter

- Clean the lens.
- Turn off the flash.
- Tap to focus on the product, then slide exposure down slightly so white and silver prints keep detail.
- Use the 2x lens (or step back and zoom) to avoid the stretched look of the wide lens on small items.
- Shoot in good light rather than night mode; layer lines and engraving detail look sharper.

## 3. The shot list for every listing

1. **Hero shot:** the product alone on a clean background, filling most of the frame.
2. **Scale shot:** in a hand or next to a familiar object (mug, phone, coin).
3. **Detail shot:** close-up of layer quality, engraving depth or finish.
4. **Lifestyle shot:** in use: a planter on a shelf, a cutting board in a kitchen, an ornament on a tree.
5. **Variants:** all colours or sizes together.
6. **Packaging shot:** in the gift box or mailer, if you offer one.

Keep your photos honest. Show real layer lines and real colours; buyers forgive visible layers far more than they forgive a product that looks different from the listing.

## 4. Make packaging look professional

A branded box makes a $15 print look like a gift. A packaging mockup tool such as [Pacdora](/en/review/pacdora/) lets you preview a box design in 3D for your listing photos and download a dieline you can print, or score and cut on your own laser.

## 5. Shoot short videos (they get the most reach)

Vertical 9:16 video works on TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Pinterest.

- **The process clip:** 3 to 5 seconds of the printer laying the first layers or the laser engraving, filmed on a phone clamp.
- **The reveal:** pulling the print off the bed or peeling the masking tape off an engraving.
- **The 360:** put the product on a cheap rotating display stand and film a slow turn.
- **The in-use clip:** a hand picking it up and using it.

Film several short clips, then let an editor do the rest. [InVideo AI](/en/review/invideo-ai/) can turn your clips into a captioned vertical video with music and a voiceover from a text prompt. Use your own footage instead of stock clips so the video shows the real product.

## 6. Edit lightly

- Crop straight and centre the product.
- Correct white balance so white filament looks white, not blue or yellow.
- Remove dust spots, but do not hide real defects.
- Export at the size each platform asks for (square or 4:5 for listings, 2:3 for Pinterest pins, 9:16 for video).

## 7. Post on a schedule

Batch a week of content at once: one photo, one process clip and one customer photo per day. A scheduler such as [Vista Social](/en/review/vista-social/) can post the same product to Pinterest (with a board and link), Instagram, TikTok and Facebook from one calendar.

## 8. Where to send the traffic

Every pin and post should link to the product page on your [Shopify store](/en/review/shopify-for-makers/) or [Etsy shop](/en/review/etsy-for-makers/). Not sure which? Read our [Shopify vs Etsy for 3D print sellers](/en/compare/shopify-vs-etsy-for-3d-print-sellers/) comparison.

## Quick checklist

- Window light, diffused, with a white bounce card
- Clean background and a lifestyle surface
- Hero, scale, detail, lifestyle, variants, packaging
- One 9:16 process or reveal clip
- Honest colours and layer lines
- Scheduled posts linking to the product page
