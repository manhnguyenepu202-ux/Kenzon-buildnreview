Black Friday pages for 3D printers, lasers and scanners are a sea of crossed-out prices and big percentages. When we built our [Black Friday deals page](/en/black-friday/), we compared every price with what the same product cost on the same store a week earlier. Of 37 discounted products, **only 11 were actually cheaper** than seven days before. The rest were showing their normal price next to a list price nobody pays.

> **Key takeaways**
> - A crossed-out "list price" is not evidence of a deal. Many maker brands show one all year.
> - The only reliable test is price history: is it cheaper than it was last week or last month?
> - Sales also end and prices rise. Check before you assume a price will still be there.
> - Compare the full cost: bundles, accessories, software, and the variant you actually need.

## Why the percentage is usually meaningless

Many brands in this market sell almost everything below a high "list price" for months. A laser module listed at $459 and always sold at $149 is described as "68% off", but nothing has changed. The number tells you how high the list price is, not how good today’s price is.

That doesn’t mean the price is bad. The [Snapmaker 10W laser module](/en/review/snapmaker-10w-laser-module/) at $149 is a good price for what it does. It just isn’t a Black Friday price.

## What a real deal looks like

A real deal is a price that is lower than the product’s own recent price. In our check:

- The [LaserPecker LX2](/en/review/laserpecker-lx2/) 20W bundle went from $1,499 to **$1,299**: $200 cheaper in a week.
- The [Snapmaker U1](/en/review/snapmaker-u1/) went from $749 to **$699**.
- Several LaserPecker portables dropped by $20 to $60.

Those are genuine cuts. On our deals page they carry a "Price drop" label, separate from the store’s crossed-out price.

## Prices go up, too

Sales end. In the same week, the [EINSTAR 2](/en/review/einstar-2/) 3D scanner went from $899 to $1,249 on the official store, and the rest of the EINSTAR range rose by $380 to $400. If you had read a review quoting the old price, you would have been in for a surprise at checkout. We have since updated those reviews and lowered their value scores.

The lesson: a price in a review or a social media post is only true on the day it was checked. Always confirm on the store.

## A five-minute checklist before you buy

1. **Check the price history.** Price trackers, our deals page, or simply a screenshot from last week. If it hasn’t moved, it isn’t a Black Friday deal.
2. **Check the variant.** Stores often advertise the cheapest configuration: the smallest laser module, the kit without the colour system, the bundle without a chiller. Make sure the price matches the version you need.
3. **Check what is missing.** Air assist, honeycomb beds, enclosures, goggles, chillers and software are often extra. LightBurn alone costs $99 to $199.
4. **Check price protection.** Some stores refund the difference if the price falls again before Black Friday. If yours does, buying early is low risk.
5. **Check returns and support.** For big machines, a 30-day return window and responsive support matter more than an extra 5 per cent off.

## When buying early makes sense

If a product you have already chosen shows a real price drop, and the store offers price protection, there is little reason to wait. If the "deal" is the same price it has been for months, waiting costs you nothing either: there is a fair chance the real discount comes closer to November 27.

## The bottom line

Ignore the percentage, check the history, and price the whole setup rather than the headline unit. Do that and Black Friday becomes a good time to buy maker tools instead of a stressful guessing game.
